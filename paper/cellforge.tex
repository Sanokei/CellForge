\begin{abstract}
We ask whether large language model (LLM) agents can build useful neural computation by
\emph{directly writing} weights, biases and wiring, with no gradient-based fitting of the
resulting artifact, and whether small authored cells can then be recombined by breeding and
evolution. CellForge provides a recursive graph representation (primitive cells composed into
nested, shareable bundles with optional conditional dispatch), atomic validated transactions,
a typed \texttt{import\_def} crossover, an append-only hash-chained ledger, and a final
evaluation on a separate machine with freshly generated cases.
On variable-length digit strings, four independently authored artifacts---including one
written with no metric feedback---read in-distribution final cases perfectly (exact match
1.000, $n=2000$); under distribution shift the best reads 73.8\% of strings in an unseen font (72.3\% under size shift; the others 38--57\%),
against 10.0\% for a gradient-trained slot-MLP control (83.8\% in distribution) and 0\% for random direct edits.
Authored character language models reach 0.678--0.681 bits per character on a synthetic
grammar, but authored and control models score near chance (0.224--0.266) on public
multiple-choice probes. On ARC-AGI-2 public-training development tasks, authored
graph cells solve 3/200 tasks and 4/200 with one object gene; breeding, mutation and random search
mostly tie, and an evolved cellular-automaton control solves 0/40. These are development
results on exposed data, from single small runs, with no private-set score. A later four-author
literal-tensor batch reaches 1/4 search and 0/4 development tasks, matching a hand-coded baseline.
Separately, direct per-task Codex calls solve 4/4 previously exposed search tasks and 7/7
newly selected public-training tasks. Across 12 planned tasks, 11 were scored and one was
cancelled before author creation. The 11/11 scored result is a small, nonrepresentative sample
with unverified model prior exposure, not a benchmark score or a literal-weight result.
On a separate synthetic spatial rule family, two founders reach 24/24 held-out cases but implement
one functional behavior. A two-specialist bank then solves 24/24 fresh synthetic tasks versus
12/24 for either specialist; its authored router is exactly fixed identity routing, which also
solves 24/24. These results show bounded parameter transfer and composition within host-written
algorithms, not learned-router superiority, sustained evolution, or general intelligence.
Two fixed-T8 studies yield identity-duplicate founders (0/17 exposed training demonstrations),
a mutation timeout and an explicit-objective founder timeout. Missing candidates and uncomputed
paired randoms leave both two-attempt numerical comparisons incomplete; the prompt-wording effect
is unestimated.
Dispatcher checks are operational evidence only. Exact native-author credit use remains unknown,
and the full literal-weight qualification gates remain unsuccessful.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Neural networks are almost always \emph{fitted}: an optimizer adjusts parameters to reduce a
loss on data. LLMs, however, can read images, reason about mechanisms and write precise
numerical specifications. This suggests a different construction process: an LLM
\emph{authors} a network the way an engineer writes a program, observes its failures and
revises it. CellForge is an experimental system for testing this idea, and for testing whether
small authored modules (\emph{cells}), grouped into \emph{bundles} and \emph{circuits}, can be
joined by \emph{breeding} (typed crossover of imported definitions) and \emph{evolution}
(discrete mutation and selection). Everything is pure in the sense of the project owner's rule:
no gradients of any kind, including fitness-weighted gradient estimators, are used in primary
arms. The target hardware is low power: two CPU cores and 4\,GB of RAM.

Our primary question is whether LLM agents can discover useful neural computations, their
recursive organization and better ways of developing them, using evidence-backed memory and
direct numerical and structural edits, producing an offline artifact that improves on
independent evaluation.

\paragraph{How results are validated.} In the early experiments, development data, reference anchors and all
diagnostics are author-visible and count as development feedback. Final cases are rendered on a
separate server by an \texttt{evaluator} account with a fresh secret seed per evaluation, and
only aggregates leave the machine. Every run records hashes, ledger entries and a receipt file
under \texttt{runs/}; each claim below names its receipt where one exists. Many later experiments
use exposed public development tasks for which no final split exists; those are labelled
development results throughout. Section~\ref{sec:native20261006} separately reports later local
experiments whose final synthetic panels were procedurally sealed until search selection; these
were not separate-server or adversarially audited evaluations. This revision integrates the
specified October reports and preserves historical results; it is not a new audit of every
server campaign in \href{https://github.com/Sanokei/CellForge/blob/02beaf18251fca5ed1f1eb55e92f00a3e3820e88/LAB.md}{\texttt{LAB.md}}.

\paragraph{Scope and non-goals.}
The artifacts studied are an offline image-to-text recognizer, small authored byte-level
language models, and authored grid-transformation graphs. None has language understanding,
dialogue, speech capability or general intelligence. The project owner's long-term aim is more
ambitious (a conversational, multimodal system whose computation is constructed rather than
fitted); we state it only to explain design choices (modality-agnostic decoder, recursive
reusable components) and keep it strictly separate from our claims. Section~\ref{sec:roadmap}
lists what would have to be demonstrated, in order.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
(1) A recursive cell/bundle IR with lazy conditional dispatch, content hashing,
behaviour-preserving refactors and validity-preserving typed crossover; (2) a transaction and
governance design that makes each parameter change traceable to an accepted proposal and
separates development feedback from independent evaluation; (3) measurements of
LLM-authored artifacts against gradient-trained and random-search controls on digit strings,
synthetic and real text, MNIST-1D, public multiple-choice probes, and ARC-AGI-2 development
tasks, including several negative results; (4) a later literal-tensor study distinguishing an
ARC development null, synthetic rule transfer, identity-router composition, a bounded shared-NCA
null with a terminal author timeout, and operational dispatch checks. These are separate evidence classes, not successive improvements on one benchmark.

\section{Related work}
Architecture search composes motifs hierarchically \cite{liu2018hierarchical} and evolves
topologies and components \cite{miikkulainen2017evolving}; both fit weights by training.
Our evidence memory adapts ideas from visual retrieval-augmented generation
\cite{yu2024visrag,sun2025visrag2,wang2026vimrag}. Neural cellular automata have been applied
to ARC-AGI with a separate gradient-trained model per task \cite{xu2025nca}; our evolved
variant (Section~\ref{sec:nca}) replaces gradients by discrete selection and is a different
experiment. Tracr compiles hand-written programs into transformer weights without gradient
descent \cite{tracr}, the closest prior example of directly constructed weights that we
recorded. A recorded report of LLM-seeded genetic search over fixed-width programs on ARC-AGI-2
\cite{gensearch} motivates our validity-preserving genome; we did not independently
verify its numbers and do not compare against them. We are not aware of prior work in which
LLMs author all weights of a working recognizer without fitting, but a wider prior-art review is
required before any novelty claim.

\paragraph{ARC harness context (primary sources checked 6 October 2026).}
ARC Prize publishes MIT-licensed benchmarking harnesses for ARC-AGI-1/2 and ARC-AGI-3
\cite{arc12harness,arc3harness}. Its Astra results list best observed ARC-AGI-1 accuracy of
98.5\% (high or xhigh) and ARC-AGI-2 accuracy of 95.0\% (max). For ARC-AGI-3 semi-private,
the Provider Adapter reaches 99.95\% at high reasoning (\$18{,}817 total), versus 62.71\%
at max reasoning (\$26{,}098 total) with Standard \cite{astraresults,astrablog}.
These are different reasoning settings and harnesses, not a controlled estimate of the harness
effect alone. The Standard interface retains model-selected notes; the Provider Adapter also
retains opaque reasoning state and uses compaction \cite{astrablog}. ARC-AGI-3 reports action
efficiency, not percent of games solved; its percentages must not be substituted for our level
counts or exact-grid task accuracy. Benchmark near-saturation is not proof of AGI
\cite{astrablog}.

Poetiq's MIT-licensed solver executes proposed programs on demonstrations and builds subsequent
prompts from their errors and scores \cite{poetiqcode}. Its December 2025 report records an
ARC Prize-verified 54\% on ARC-AGI-2 semi-private at \$30.57 per problem
\cite{poetiqverified}; this is a dated result, not a current ranking claim. Tycho's Apache-2.0
repository describes executable world-model construction, checking and planning, and reports
100.00 RHAE on 25 public ARC-AGI-3 games with GPT-5.6 Sol and Claude Opus 5
\cite{tycho}. The community leaderboard classifies such public scores as self-reported unless
otherwise indicated \cite{arccommunity}. Public and verified semi-private scores are not
interchangeable. These systems motivate explicit memory, feedback and verification ablations;
they do not demonstrate CellForge's literal-weight learning or evolutionary mechanism.

\subsection{Automated research and language-model reward design}\label{sec:rewardrelated}
The following comparison distinguishes the artifact an LLM writes from the mechanism
that changes policy weights. These studies inform experimental design; none establishes
a CellForge result or demonstrates its literal-weight population standard.

\paragraph{Autoresearch as training-code search.}
Karpathy's \texttt{autoresearch} permits edits to \texttt{train.py}, while
\texttt{program.md} fixes the data/evaluation code in \texttt{prepare.py}
\cite{autoresearchcode}. Each trial has five minutes of training on one GPU,
excluding startup/compilation, and the agent retains changes according to validation
bits per byte. The inspected training implementation uses backpropagation with
Muon/AdamW. The LLM therefore searches training programs and settings; an optimizer
fits model weights. Repeatedly inspected validation scores are search feedback,
not an untouched final test. A per-trial time limit alone does not equate total
search effort, hardware, agent tokens or retries. The cited branch was inspected
on 8 October 2026; its commit remains unpinned.

\paragraph{ENPIRE as a physical feedback harness.}
ENPIRE first constructs safety, reset and verification interfaces with human feedback,
then freezes them for policy improvement \cite{enpire2026}. The second stage supports
heuristics, generated policy code, behavior cloning and reinforcement learning.
Its headline 99\% success must be read under the paper's eight-retry protocol,
which permits recovery informed by earlier failure; it is not pass@1 or independent
best-of-eight sampling. Appendix A.2 describes an LLM-written binary verifier from
labeled success/failure examples, constrained by accuracy and latency requirements.
The agent cannot change metric computation but may inspect failures; that feedback
exposure must remain explicit when interpreting the described held-out set.
The relevant transfer to CellForge is a fixed interface and independently checked
success signal, not a claim that the LLM directly authors all policy tensors.
The paper and official code repository are separate cited sources; no robot
experiment was reproduced here \cite{enpirerepo}.

\paragraph{Eureka as reward-program search.}
Eureka generates and revises reward code from environment descriptions and training
feedback; PPO supplies the policy-weight updates \cite{eureka2023}. It reports
outperforming human rewards on 83\% of 29 environments. That is a task-wise
comparison under its search/selection protocol, not single-proposal reliability.
The method uses five independent search restarts, each with five iterations of
sixteen proposals. Intermediate rewards receive one PPO run, with acknowledged
noise; final rewards receive five runs. Main results select the best search
outcomes, and policy evaluation uses maximum-checkpoint metrics. These selection
and repetition details matter when comparing an evolutionary author against random
edits under a finite budget.

\paragraph{Text2Reward as dense shaping code.}
Text2Reward maps instructions and a Pythonic environment abstraction to executable
dense reward code, then trains policies using PPO or SAC \cite{text2reward2024}.
It reports comparable or better success and convergence than expert rewards on
13 of 17 manipulation tasks. Execution feedback repairs code errors, while human
feedback can refine intended behavior. Perception is supplied by other components,
and the paper notes that symbolic rewards may omit relevant aspects of the goal.
Thus a valid program need not capture user intent, and reward synthesis remains
distinct from directly writing policy weights.

\paragraph{Implications for the proposed CellForge study.}
Our methodological inference is to freeze the task-exact evaluator, exposure
partitions and control semantics before search. Any proposed shaping objective
should be recorded separately from final success, and must not change protected
tests or turn invalid control execution into evidence of improvement.
Account for the entire search, including rejected proposals, retries and independent
seed repetitions, rather than comparing only winning artifacts. Preserve first-attempt
accuracy alongside any retry-assisted result and freeze selection before final access.
For the literal-weight claim, keep host source fixed and expose only permitted
training failures to the author; separately label any reward-code or algorithm
search. These are proposed safeguards, not imported empirical gains, execution
authorization or permission to reopen closed campaigns.


\section{System}\label{sec:system}
\subsection{Representation}
The following graph and operator descriptions document the early experimental protocols.
The later \href{https://github.com/Sanokei/CellForge/blob/02beaf18251fca5ed1f1eb55e92f00a3e3820e88/docs/ONE_STANDARD.md}{literal-weight standard} requires LLM-authored
founders and mutations, joint cell/router tensors, matched charged random edits, crossover,
and improvement across generations. Early gene selection, count-fitted seeds and automated
numeric search remain baselines under that standard; their presence in this historical paper
does not qualify them as the current primary experiment. Later spatial cells use tensors to
parameterize host-written algorithms, so ``weight'' does not by itself imply a learned neural
implementation of those algorithms.

A \emph{cell} computes $h=\sigma(W_1x+b_1)$, $y=W_2h+b_2$ over the last axis of its input.
A \emph{bundle} is a typed DAG whose nodes are parameter-free operations, parameter tensors,
cells, other bundles, or a top-1 \texttt{dispatch} among bundles; unselected branches do not
execute. The same representation is used at every depth; definitions may be shared and
parameter sharing is explicit. Recursive template references and exponential expansion are
rejected at validation. A model has an \texttt{encode} bundle (pixels $\to$ memory) and a
\texttt{step} bundle (memory, step index, previous token, state $\to$ logits, state), driven
by a bounded greedy decoder that stops on the model's own EOS. Structural profiles (ladder,
ladder-micro, open) constrain organization and are checked by a validator.

\subsection{Construction protocol and maker operator}
Makers submit JSON proposals of atomic operations (set parameter by explicit value or by a
deterministic expansion of an author-written pattern; put/delete definitions; wrap/unwrap).
Proposals are validated against their exact base hash, executed on sample inputs and, for pure
refactors, tested for exact behavioural equivalence. Makers may not run code that computes
weights from data, may not script search loops, and receive development feedback only through a
budgeted \texttt{dev-eval} command. A manual-maker operator records development train-pair
diagnostics, authored transactions, actor/model provenance, graph hashes and candidate status.
Each serializable proposal consumes a budget unit, including invalid, rejected and unchanged
edits; transactions are applied to a copy and checked against graph validation, structural
profiles and typed runtime fixtures. Three focused tests pass. The operator calls no LLM
service and performs no benchmark itself, so a matched non-LLM mutation arm must use the same
proposal budget and access. A frozen candidate receives one ledger-accounted final call,
consumed even on failure. This is accounting infrastructure, not capability.

\subsection{Breeding and evolution}
Breeding uses checked \texttt{import\_def} crossover: a child imports definitions from
parents with namespace, shape, ownership and resource validation. The proposed and later
implemented genome uses fixed slots with typed ports, bounded parameter shapes and shared
parameter identities; a slot may exchange only a compatible definition, and crossover preserves
dependency order, declared state and the two-attempt grid interface. Broken wiring should be
prevented by construction; static validation remains a final safeguard and does not show task
competence. Children commit parent hashes, seeds and donor-per-slot choices, and neutral lineage
metadata does not change canonical genotype identity. Primary selection is discrete $(\mu,\lambda)$
population selection with Gaussian coordinate or block mutation; it is not a fitness-weighted
gradient estimate. The owner later authorized automated numeric fitness selection and count-fitted
seeds (dev-training corpus only), and then prohibited gradients of every kind and excluded the
Hedge-style adaptive mixer from all primary arms. Evolved parameters are therefore reported as
fitness-selected rather than hand-authored, with donor provenance preserved.

\subsection{Governance and evaluation boundary}
A hash-chained ledger, graph version store and receipts make each run auditable; reported
ledger-entry counts were verified after retrieval. Final cases for the digit task exclude, for
strings of length $\geq4$, every string authors ever saw; the unseen-font split uses a font file
never present on the authoring machine. Reserved audit data must never be shown to a maker, and
schema guards alone cannot prove information isolation.

\section{Experiments}
Unless stated otherwise each experiment is a single run per arm.

\paragraph{Results.} Table~\ref{tab:r1}. All four authored artifacts are perfect in
distribution, using two or fewer development evaluations each, so the in-distribution task is
saturated and does not discriminate arms. Every maker converged on the same mechanism family
(vertical alignment, left-edge detection, a fixed 12\,px character pitch, hand-typed
15$\times$12 glyph templates), yet small decisions produced large robustness differences:
artifact A's normalized agreement score and $\pm3$\,px search tolerance generalized best;
artifact B hard-coded eight character cells and failed every 9-digit string. LLM authors can

training: the control lacks translation invariance and a gradient-trained convolutional network
would likely be perfect in distribution. The templates came from \emph{looking at} development
images; we regard the author's visual perception as the channel through which learning happens
and report it as such.

\subsection{Synthetic-grammar hierarchical mixture-of-experts language models (\texttt{tinylm/1})}\label{sec:exp2}
The owner judged digit recognition too easy a test of the intended cell$\to$cluster$\to$expert
design, so authors built character-level language models with routing and recurrent state doing
real work.

\paragraph{Task and arms.} Text comes from a private English-like grammar (12 nouns, 12 verbs,
agreement that must survive relative clauses, adjectives, prepositions, adverbs, ``, and''

development documents, never the grammar. Vocabulary: 32 tokens; the model receives the last
eight characters, the position and its own state. Scoring is bits per character (bpc) under
teacher forcing on fresh documents, plus grammaticality of 200 free-running samples
(temperature 0.8) judged by the private recognizer. Three authors used an enforced ladder
profile (cells inside clusters, clusters inside experts, a router driving lazy top-1 dispatch
among at least two experts), two an open profile. Controls fitted from data on the same 2{,}000
documents: uniform, $n$-gram counts and a gradient-trained MLP over the same eight-character
window (Adam, 74k parameters).

\paragraph{Results.} Table~\ref{tab:exp2}. All five authored models reach 0.678--0.681 bpc,
within 0.004 bpc (under 1\%) of the generator's entropy, with 94--99.7\% grammatical
generations, against 0.824 bpc and 20\% for the gradient MLP. The authored models keep
singular/plural state across nested clauses in a stack held in recurrent state; the fixed-window
MLP cannot. The best model (L2) has 60 routed experts, one per grammatical situation, and only 3
of 60 sampled sentences violate the grammar. The task is exhausted: every author read roughly 40
documents, wrote the closed grammar down, hand-tallied rates and compiled it into tables and
state machines. This shows the machinery (state, recursion tracking, dispatch among tens of
experts) works end to end at 0.3--1.7M authored parameters. It does \emph{not} show that the
ladder helps: an open, unrouted model (O1) matched the ladder with three feedback runs against
five to eleven, and ladder authors were required to use routing the task does not need. Nor does
it show that authoring scales to data whose generator cannot be read off a few dozen samples.
Author-reported cost: 176--231k tokens per run.

\subsection{Real-text mixture of experts, delegation and failure-informed mutation}\label{sec:realtext}
The next step moved to real text, where no closed grammar exists. In a delegated real-text
model (hash \texttt{82ebe5e5...}), three isolated mutations reused settings from earlier failed
proposals. The first worsened development bpc from 2.4748 to 2.4826. A trigram-donor change
scored 2.4746 bpc, effectively tied with its parent; an after-period-space specialist scored
2.4743 bpc. These comparisons used the 165-document development set. The specialist was accepted
on a separate eight-expert branch; its reference-anchor bpc was 2.46152 versus 2.46236 for the
seven-expert parent over 155{,}239 tokens, with identical 0.5220 top-1 accuracy. Both checks are
development feedback and establish no final or frontier-benchmark gain; the main branch remains
seven-expert.

A UTF-8 byte lift of the eight-expert text MoE retains authored weights and 225-dimensional
recurrent state in one graph with a shared 259-token head. Its inherited experts still use only
eight projected bytes. A 387-token ASCII compatibility probe preserves 208 correct predictions,
with loss changing from 2.4798989 bits per character to 2.4803443 bits per byte. This
establishes input plumbing, not language competence; three ordinary or Unicode prompts
repeatedly emit ``too'' to the token limit (receipts: \texttt{runs/unified-byte}). A directly
authored repetition-memory mutation reading a 64-token context has parent loss 2.480344 versus
2.512589 bits per byte on the same 387 bytes; both predict 208 tokens correctly with zero scoring
failures, and both answer 0/4 author-visible novel key/value binding prompts exactly. The mutation
was rejected for promotion (\path{runs/parallel-reasoning/DECISION.json}); this small
development probe is not a held-out reasoning evaluation.

generator source, a restriction that was procedural rather than technically enforced.

\paragraph{Crossover.} A fourth maker imported the independently authored A1 and A2 filter banks
into a child graph and authored a combining rule. After five development evaluations the accepted
child scored 0.5295 on fresh final signals, an absolute gain of 0.064 over the better parent; it
uses about 29.7 million multiply--accumulates over the final set versus 13.3 million for A1 and
16.4 million for A2. This is one selected offspring, not evidence of a general evolutionary
advantage. A three-parent extension scored 0.572 on the development set and 0.557 on the
reference anchor ($n=1000$ each), then 0.5360 on a separate fresh-seed final set ($n=2000$). Its
apparent 0.0065 gain over the two-parent child is not reliable: the final evaluations used
different seeds, the intervals overlap substantially, and the third parent raises final-set
computation from 29.7 to 39.3 million multiply--accumulates. A version-2 diagnostic plugin
reporting class confusion and wrong-prediction margins was validated and activated, but
fresh-maker Round B under that harness was never evaluated, so no harness-effect claim is
possible. New MNIST-1D jobs were paused under the later document-benchmark priority.

\subsection{Frontier-benchmark probes at chance}\label{sec:frontier}
We adapted character-level models to multiple-choice scoring (\texttt{mc-loglik/1}) by summing
next-character negative log likelihood over each answer choice, also reporting length-normalized
scoring. The vocabulary lowercases text and omits many capitals, digits and symbols, so coverage
is imperfect. MMLU test, ARC-Challenge test and HellaSwag validation were staged on the private
evaluation server; GPQA was unavailable because access requires dataset authorization.

The first sweep covers a synthetic-grammar authored model and three real-text controls
(unigram, trigram, small gradient MLP), each on a fixed 1{,}000-item subset per benchmark.
Length-normalized accuracies span 0.243--0.266 on MMLU, 0.224--0.248 on ARC-Challenge and
0.230--0.264 on HellaSwag. The delegated real-text MoE (\texttt{82ebe5e5...}) on 1{,}000 items
each scores 0.241 MMLU, 0.243 ARC-Challenge and 0.241 HellaSwag (Wilson intervals
$[0.2155,0.2685]$, $[0.2174,0.2705]$, $[0.2155,0.2685]$; character coverage 0.9147, 0.9624,
0.9889; receipt \path{runs/bench_results/server-aggregate-20260930.jsonl}). All values sit
around the four-choice chance rate of 0.25 and provide no evidence of frontier benchmark
competence; they are negative transfer evidence and a test of the scoring interface, not a
comparison to frontier language models.

\subsection{Control execution must remain distinguishable from task failure}\label{sec:controlintegrity}
Read-only inspection of source at commit
\href{https://github.com/Sanokei/CellForge/tree/02beaf18251fca5ed1f1eb55e92f00a3e3820e88}{\texttt{02beaf18}}

\href{https://github.com/Sanokei/CellForge/blob/02beaf18251fca5ed1f1eb55e92f00a3e3820e88/scripts/structcode.py#L248-L270}{\texttt{structcode.evaluate}},
the zero and random control calls retain fit counts but discard their error lists.
An execution error can consequently appear as a low-scoring control and support the
\texttt{controls\_ok} flag. This is a prospective acceptance defect: weight dependence
requires valid, correctly shaped counterfactual execution, not failure to execute.
The \href{https://github.com/Sanokei/CellForge/blob/02beaf18251fca5ed1f1eb55e92f00a3e3820e88/reports/CAPABILITY_WEIGHTS_CHECKPOINT_20261006.md}{capability checkpoint}
already requires preserving these errors before new proposals.

Separately, the pinned
\href{https://github.com/Sanokei/CellForge/blob/02beaf18251fca5ed1f1eb55e92f00a3e3820e88/tests/standard_checks.py#L130-L133}{\texttt{standard\_checks.\_zeros\_like}}
recurses through lists but maps a dictionary to scalar zero.
\href{https://github.com/Sanokei/CellForge/blob/02beaf18251fca5ed1f1eb55e92f00a3e3820e88/tests/arc_goal_checks.py#L134-L153}{\texttt{check\_weights\_matter}}

\section{Discussion}
\paragraph{What worked.} Direct authoring produced working recognizers and compact language
machinery without gradients: digit strings read perfectly in distribution, with one artifact
retaining 73.8\% under an unseen font, and authored synthetic-grammar models reached within 0.004
bpc of the generator entropy. Typed crossover retained parental building blocks on tiny probes and
lifted MNIST-1D accuracy by 0.064 in one selected child. Narrow authored components (typed transfer,
binding, deduction, addition, action grammar) execute exactly within their grammars, and a compiled
backend gives large verified speedups. The later native studies add synthetic transfer within a
supplied spatial family and composition of two specialists. Matching the hand-coded reference
and fixed identity router sharply limits what those positive results explain.
The separate direct-prediction baselines solve four exposed search cases and seven newly selected
public-training cases. Their descriptive 11/11 scored (12 planned, one cancelled) demonstrates
per-task inference success on a small,
nonrepresentative sample, without proving unseen-task transfer or a reusable learned-weight artifact.

\paragraph{What did not.} Authored and control character models sit at chance on public
multiple-choice probes; the byte language graph emits invalid actions and repeats tokens; document
evaluation is blocked on language capability and on an unresolved related-edition overlap; evolution
from authored founders tied random search in the language mini-pilot and in most ARC pilots; dense
synthetic success (300/300) did not transfer to DEV200, where every portfolio remains at 3/200--4/200;
and evolved NCAs solve 0/40. The gradient MLP beat the best MNIST-1D authored model. The later
four-author spatial batch again has zero ARC development gain. The fixed-T8 founder is an
identity duplicate with zero exact demonstrations; its sole mutation times out, leaving the
second random comparison uncomputed. A fresh original-prompt founder also duplicates identity,
while its explicit-objective counterpart times out. That prompt comparison is inconclusive;
these studies add no positive shared-weight result and do not resolve representational capacity.
Neither successful synthetic
batch required a mutation, neither demonstrated crossover, and no sustained evolutionary
improvement follows from them.

\paragraph{Honest limits.} Independent server final evaluations in the early experiments exist only
for digits, synthetic grammar and MNIST-1D; their ARC, byte-language and document results are
development results on exposed public data. The later synthetic final panels were local and
procedurally sealed, with author isolation parent-attested, and do not establish ARC accuracy.
Runs are single small runs (one per arm in most cases), confidence intervals are wide, and the two-task
gaps in Table~\ref{tab:pop} are not evidence. Authors and orchestrator share an operating-system account
locally, and the orchestrator has administrative access to the evaluation server, so the final-evaluation
boundary is physical rather than adversarially audited. Token usage is self-reported; the digit task used a
single font family in development; the visual-retrieval memory arm and the MNIST-1D harness-version
comparison were never run; the MNIST-1D crossover has one independently tested child and no random-crossover
control, so its advantage may reflect selection and extra computation; a co-researcher's library results
and scratch NCA numbers are not reproduced by us. The authored models transfer competence from the author
LLM's pretraining and must be reported as such. There is no private-set score, no official ARC-AGI-2 or
BFCL score, the system is not an LLM, and no claim of general intelligence or frontier superiority is made.

\paragraph{Design-only pretrained composition.} A System~1/System~2 proposal
(\path{docs/SYSTEM12_DESIGN.md}) would compose pretrained models (a gradient-trained 0.6B-parameter
decision-head system and a pretrained generative candidate) with evolved routing. It would import
pretrained capability, is separate from the no-gradient line, and was not executed: no model was
downloaded, and the approximately 4.34GB required files exceed the reported 3.5GB free disk.

\section{Roadmap beyond the current results}\label{sec:roadmap}
\paragraph{Next ARC-AGI-1/2/3 agenda (proposed, not executed here).}
For ARC-AGI-1/2, retain a task-ID-deduplicated public-training manifest and explicit exposure
history; freeze search, selection and untouched reporting roles before proposing reusable cells.
Test whether synthetic gains transfer to new authorized task families against zero tensors,
matched random attempts, fixed identity routing and the same hand-coded reference. Report task
accuracy and failures with original denominators, rather than combining historical split scores.
For ARC-AGI-3, the documented
\href{https://github.com/Sanokei/CellForge/blob/02beaf18251fca5ed1f1eb55e92f00a3e3820e88/reports/ARC3_SHARED_PROBE_20261002.md}{shared-policy probe} solved zero levels on
three public development games at 1{,}500 actions each after a runtime-only repair, with all
valid controls also at zero. The next hypothesis should target action-effect inference and
no-op recovery using one shared history-conditioned policy, with matched action budgets and
history/blind ablations; game-ID routing or another game-specific campaign would not test that
hypothesis. New-game claims require genuinely unused games, which these reports do not supply.

Across all three, establish diverse behavior, failure-informed literal mutations, joint
cell/router crossover and retained gains across multiple seeds and generations under one
predeclared accounting scheme. Host algorithm changes, inference-time program synthesis and
authored tensor changes must retain separate provenance and controls. Official harnesses can
inform protocol design, but their provider-model scores do not predict an offline bank's score.
The ARC-AGI-2 evaluation exclusion remains; private or semi-private evaluation services must
not become an iterative tuning channel \cite{arcpolicy}. This agenda authorizes no run, new
data access, submission or expenditure by itself.

\subsection{A falsifiable next shared-weight hypothesis}
The completed fixed-T8 tranche in Section~\ref{sec:ncatranche} did not demonstrate improvement:
its founder duplicated identity and no mutation tensor was returned. The separate
prompt comparison (Section~\ref{sec:ncaprompt}) likewise leaves the explicit-objective arm
unmeasured after timeout. These limited results do not answer the broader question below or
authorize another call. Any subsequent study would require a new prospectively frozen protocol.

\begin{samepage}
The remaining research question is whether training-only failure feedback can improve a
\emph{single reusable literal-weight artifact} while its host computation remains fixed.
A proposed comparison is
\[
\Delta_{\mathrm{LLM}} =
 A(\theta_{\mathrm{LLM}},D_{\mathrm{report}})
 - A(\theta_{\mathrm{founder}},D_{\mathrm{report}}),
\]
\end{samepage}
against the same founder with matched random edits and against the simplest capable
host-written reference. Here $A$ is task-exact accuracy, $\theta$ includes the selected
cell and router tensors, and $D_{\mathrm{report}}$ is frozen and unopened until all
search-based selection ends. This notation defines a proposed estimand, not a measured
effect. Success would require a predeclared positive margin over both the founder and
matched random comparison with valid controls, plus preservation of frozen competence
anchors. Matching the host reference can support successful parameter authoring, but
cannot establish invention of its supplied algorithm.

Before authoring, the protocol must name a concrete expressive cell or typed bank,
freeze its executable source, editable tensor vocabulary, task-family manifest,
selection rule, outcome threshold, call and position caps, random seeds and stop rule.
An expressibility check must first show that the requested input distinctions survive
the representation: the reported \texttt{assoc\_induct} equal-color invariant illustrates
a case that numerical mutation cannot repair. If new structure is required, its author
and control comparison belong to a separate structure experiment.

The experiment should retain every invalid and functionally duplicate proposal in
accounting, show only training failures to authors, and select both candidate and
random winners before final access. Independent replay must strip query labels and
record valid wrong answers separately from exceptions, timeouts and malformed tensors.
A new report panel would be required because the current direct and synthetic panels
are exposed or spent. Acquisition and access must remain within existing permissions.
The direct-study STOP and both terminal NCA stops supply no further author-call budget; this proposal
starts no run.

A successful finite probe would justify testing repeated failure-driven mutation and
joint cell/router crossover across seeds. It would not itself satisfy the broader
\texttt{ONE\_STANDARD} population requirements, demonstrate ARC-1/2/3 unification or
establish general intelligence. Direct solver calls should remain a separate baseline:
their different task panels, model identities/reasoning settings and budgets do not
constitute a controlled causal comparison with the tensor studies.

\paragraph{Longer-term goals retained from the original draft.}
The path toward the long-term aim requires, in order: robust alphanumeric recognition across unseen font
families; real-world text lines competitive with a parameter-matched gradient-trained CNN (the critical
go/no-go test, since authoring effort scales with case diversity rather than data); constructed sequence
priors; measured component reuse across visual tasks; constructed algorithmic reasoning with length
generalization; and only then language. The largest risks are scale (capable language models have
$10^8$--$10^{12}$ parameters, far beyond hand authoring, pushing toward weight \emph{generators}) and
provenance of linguistic competence. For the ARC line the owner's priorities are a valid open entry, stronger
authored object, recoloring, counting and path cells tested on dense generators, and matched ablations of
breeding mechanisms.
